## Workshop materials (added)

**Focus:** the session tests how a student approaches a problem, not what they've memorised. The lecturer's original words: "The technical interview is not about can you solve the problem they give to you, it is about how you approach & try to solve the problem. Ask clarifying questions, state your assumptions, talk aloud about different options. Listen to the interviewers, explain your decisions. Be curious, don't pretend to know things if you don't."

The slides use a slightly adjusted version, checked against published interview advice. The mindset line reads "not **only** about whether you can solve the problem", and the five habits are:
1. **Clarify the problem**: ask questions, then state and check your assumptions.
2. **Think out loud**: compare two or three options and their trade-offs.
3. **Explain your decisions**: say why you chose it; listen to what the interviewer tells you, and use it.
4. **Start simple, then improve and check it**: get a basic version, improve it, then test it against edge cases.
5. **Be honest about what you don't know**: say so, then reason from what you do know.

- **Audience:** mostly 1st and 2nd year students. There are no year levels. The guide notes what a reasonable early-year answer looks like compared with a strong one.
- **Questions:** 36 open-ended technical CS problems, 6 in each of 6 tracks, with details deliberately left out. Every question has a public source showing it (or a close variant) is asked in real interviews; the source is on each card in the facilitator guide, not on the deck. No two questions test the same core problem. Tracks: Algorithms (A1–A6), Systems & software (S1–S6), Data & databases (D1–D6), AI & ML (M1–M6), Scenarios (SC1–SC6, workplace situations that need technical judgement), Your experience (E1–E6). The board shows one track per row.
- **Format:** round robin, one student at a time. Each student sees the briefing slides, then a problem is drawn. They get **1 minute to think** and **3 minutes to answer out loud**, and may ask the lecturer questions at any time.
  - **No hints.** The lecturer answers clarifying questions but gives no hints; the slides already lay out the approach.
  - The lecturer may optionally add a new constraint partway through, to see whether the student listens.
  - Then verbal feedback.
- **No scoring.** Feedback only, built around the five habits.
- `presentation.html` is what students see (7 slides): welcome, mindset, five habits, how this works, when you're stuck, question board, takeaways (with a **Next student** button).
  - Open it in a browser and press F11 for full screen.
  - On the board, use the track filter, type the student's name, then press **D** to draw.
  - "Mark done and close" greys a question out. Click "Clear used marks" before the first student. The board has no "not yet drawn" count, only that button.
- `facilitator_guide.md` is for the lecturer only.
  - For each question: hidden details to reveal only when asked, good clarifying questions, options to explore, an optional change partway through, warning signs, a stretch prompt for after the answer, and technical notes.
  - It also has a "what to look for" guide for each habit, a feedback script and a feedback notes sheet.
