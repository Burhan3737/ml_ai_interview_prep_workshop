The technical round, without the code

You'll get one open CS problem, picked at random. There's no single right answer, and no code to write. We'll talk it through the way a real technical interview would, and then you'll get feedback on how you approached it.

Data & databases
A 20 GB log file and 8 GB of RAM. Find the 10 most frequent IP addresses.
AI & ML
Your model scores 98% on training data and 60% on new data. What's going on?
Algorithms
How would you implement undo and redo in a text editor?
The technical interview is not only about whether you can solve the problem. It's about how you approach it and try to solve it.

So in this interview:

  • Questions are allowed, and expected. The problem is deliberately missing details.
  • I won't give hints. The habits on the next slide are your guide.
  • I might change something halfway through. That's on purpose.
  • You don't need to have memorised anything. Saying "I don't know that, but here's how I'd find out" is a good answer.

Five habits interviewers look for

  1. 1Clarify the problemAsk questions, then state and check your assumptions."How much data are we talking about? I'll assume it's one user at a time."
  2. 2Think out loudCompare two or three options and their trade-offs."We could use a list or a hash map. The list is simpler, but…"
  3. 3Explain your decisionsSay why you chose it. Listen to what the interviewer tells you, and use it."Since you said it's mostly reads, I'd pick B because…"
  4. 4Start simple, then improve and check itGet a basic version, improve it, then test it against edge cases."That works. Now what if the input is empty, or huge?"
  5. 5Be honest about what you don't knowSay so, then reason from what you do know."I haven't used that. My guess is…, and I'd check by…"

When you're stuck

Say something like this

"I haven't come across that before, but here's how I'd reason about it."

"Can I check what you mean by…?"

"Let me try a small example first."

"Give me ten seconds to think."

Avoid

  • Pretending to know. Follow-up questions are designed to find it.
  • A flat "I don't know" followed by silence.
  • Going quiet for a long time while you think.
  • Forgetting to ask. If something is unclear, ask me. That's what the questions are for.

Question board

Take this with you

  • It's about the approachInterviewers watch how you think, not only whether you reach the answer.
  • Clarify before you solveAsk questions and say your assumptions out loud. It saves you from solving the wrong problem.
  • Check your answerBefore you say you're done, test it on an example and an edge case.
  • Always have a second optionThen explain why you chose one over the other.
  • Listen closelyWhen the interviewer answers a question or changes something, use it straight away.
  • Never pretend"I don't know, but here's how I'd find out" is a strong answer.
